# Chapter 3 — Crossing Together

---

The bridge was complete now. Sounds could cross from Iro to Ua, from Ua to Iro. But there was something the bridge couldn't carry.

Iro had a sound that meant *I feel afraid*.

Ua had a sound that meant *I feel afraid too*.

But the sounds weren't the same fear. Iro's fear was about the light — the way it kept fading, the way it might one day not come back. Ua's fear was about the bridge itself — the way it might break, the way one sound might be the last.

They needed a new kind of bridge. Not a bridge for sounds, but a bridge for meanings.

## Grammar Focus: People & Family

In this chapter, you'll learn to describe **people, relationships, and emotions** in Italian.

### Essential People Vocabulary

**Family:**
- *La famiglia* - the family
- *I genitori* - the parents
- *La madre* - the mother (*Mia madre*)
- *Il padre* - the father (*Mio padre*)
- *La sorella* - the sister (*Mia sorella*)
- *Il fratello* - the brother (*Mio fratello*)
- *I figli* - the children (*I miei figli*)
- *Il marito* - the husband (*Mio marito*)
- *La moglie* - the wife (*Mia moglie*)

**People:**
- *La persona* - the person (*Una persona gentile*)
- *L'amico* - the friend (male) (*Il mio amico*)
- *L'amica* - the friend (female) (*La mia amica*)
- *Il collega* - the colleague (*Il mio collega*)
- *Il vicino* - the neighbor (*Il mio vicino*)

### Descriptive Adjectives

**Appearance:**
- *Alto* - tall (*È alto*)
- *Basso* - short (*È basso*)
- *Bello* - beautiful/handsome (*È bello*)
- *Brutto* - ugly (*È brutto*)
- *Giovane* - young (*È giovane*)
- *Vecchio* - old (*È vecchio*)
- *Biondo* - blond (*Ha i capelli biondi*)
- *Bruno* - dark-haired (*Ha i capelli castani*)

**Personality:**
- *Gentile* - kind (*È gentile*)
- *Intelligente* - intelligent (*È intelligente*)
- *Simpatico* - nice/friendly (*È molto simpatico*)
- *Allegrо* - cheerful (*È sempre allegro*)
- *Triste* - sad (*È triste oggi*)
- *Nervoso* - nervous (*È nervoso*)

### Emotions & Feelings

**Basic Emotions:**
- *Avere paura* - to be afraid (*Ho paura*)
- *Essere felice* - to be happy (*Sono felice*)
- *Essere triste* - to be sad (*Sono triste*)
- *Essere arrabbiato* - to be angry (*Sono arrabbiato*)
- *Avere fretta* - to be in a hurry (*Ho fretta*)
- *Avere sonno* - to be sleepy (*Ho sonno*)
- *Avere fame* - to be hungry (*Ho fame*)
- *Avere sete* - to be thirsty (*Ho sete*)

### Possessive Adjectives

- *Il mio / La mia* - my
- *Il tuo / La tua* - your (singular)
- *Il suo / La sua* - his/her
- *Il nostro / La nostra* - our
- *Il vostro / La vostra* - your (plural)
- *Il loro / La loro* - their

**Examples:**
- *La mia famiglia* - my family
- *Il tuo amico* - your friend
- *La nostra casa* - our house
- *I loro figli* - their children

Then Iro did something new.

Iro didn't send a sound. Iro sent a picture.

It was a picture of the light as it used to be — bright, complete, filling the world. And next to it, a picture of the light as it was now — dim, incomplete, fading.

The picture carried across the bridge.

Ua caught the picture. Ua understood — not the fear itself, but the shape of it. The *before* and the *after*. The way something changes when you can't stop it from changing.

And Ua sent a picture back.

It was a picture of the bridge as it was now — fragile, new, barely holding. And next to it, a picture of what would happen if it broke — two beings, alone again, in the fading light.

Iro caught the picture. Iro understood — not the fear itself, but the *recognition* of it. The way fear can live in two minds at once, different but the same.

## Practice Reflection

As you work through **lessons A13-A16**, you're learning to **share your inner world**:

- **Family** (Mia madre, Mio fratello) - naming who matters
- **Descriptions** (È alto, È gentile) - painting pictures with words
- **Emotions** (Ho paura, Sono felice) - building bridges of feeling
- **Possessions** (La mia casa, Il mio amico) - claiming what's yours

Your vocabulary is your gallery of pictures. Each adjective is a color, each noun a shape, each emotion a frame.

*"To understand another's fear, you must first be able to picture it. To be understood, you must be willing to show it."*
