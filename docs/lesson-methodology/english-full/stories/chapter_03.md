# Chapter 3 — Mastery: Advanced Structures and Vocabulary

## The Summit of Expression

You've climbed the mountain. The foundations are solid. The tenses are fluent. Now you stand at the summit, where the view opens in every direction.

This chapter is about **precision, nuance, and vocabulary depth** — the tools that separate B1 from B2, and B2 from C1.

**Wish & If Only** — expressing regret and desire. *"I wish I had studied harder"* (past regret). *"If only she were here"* (present longing). These structures let you express emotions that simple tenses can't capture.

**Inversion** — flipping sentence order for emphasis. *"Never have I seen such beauty."* Formal, powerful, dramatic. Used in speeches, writing, and formal communication.

**Participle Clauses** — the mark of advanced English. *"Having finished the report, she went home."* Instead of *"After she finished the report, she went home."* Shorter, more elegant, more native.

**Phrasal Verbs: Advanced** — three-part phrasals that are inseparable. *"Put up with"*, *"look forward to"*, *"run out of"*. These are the phrases native speakers use constantly.

**Preposition Patterns** — why is it *"interested IN"* but *"good AT"*? *"Responsible FOR"* but *"famous FOR"*? There's no logic — only patterns. This lesson trains those patterns.

**Idioms & Collocations** — *"make a decision"*, not *"do a decision"*. *"Take into account"*, not *"take in consideration"*. The fixed combinations that sound natural to native ears.

---

Then come the **lexical domains** — vocabulary clusters for real-world communication:

**Work & Career** — salary, deadline, promotion, interview, resign. Everything you need to navigate an English-speaking workplace.

**Meetings & Negotiation** — agenda, compromise, proposal. The language of professional interaction.

**Email & Written Communication** — regarding, attached, hereby, confirm. Formal English for business correspondence.

**Problem Solving & Opinions** — suggest, evaluate, conclude. How to think aloud in English.

**Describing & Comparing** — resemble, consist of, in contrast to. Precision in observation.

**Health & Body** — symptom, prescription, recovery. When you need a doctor.

**Travel & Transport** — boarding pass, itinerary, transfer. When you need to get somewhere.

**Technology & Digital** — upload, sync, backup. The language of the modern world.

**Money & Finance** — mortgage, investment, invoice. The language of business and personal finance.

**Relationships & Emotions** — appreciate, disappointed, rely on. The language of human connection.

---

And finally, the **integrative lessons**:

**Register: Formal vs Informal** — the same thought in three registers. Knowing *when* to be formal is as important as knowing *how*.

**News & Media** — headline, reportedly, allegedly. Understanding the news in English.

**Meta-map: Russian Cases → English** — the direct translation map. Dative → *to*, Instrumental → *with*, Prepositional → *about*. The bridge between your Russian instincts and English expression.

**Paraphrasing & Synonymy** — the final lesson. One thought, five ways to say it. This is what fluency looks like: not knowing one way, but knowing many and choosing the right one.

---

You've come a long way from *"He works from home"*. Now you can express *any* thought — at work, in life, in writing, in conversation. The summit isn't the end. It's where the real journey begins.
