# Chapter 1 — Foundations: Building Blocks

## The Garden of First Words

You stand at the gate of a garden. Inside, every flower is a sentence. But to pick them, you need to learn the rules of the garden.

**Sentence Building** teaches you the most important rule: English has a strict word order. Subject — Verb — Object. In Russian, you can scramble words freely. In English, the position IS the grammar. *"He works from home"* is correct. *"He from home works"* is broken.

**Verb BE** is the simplest and most important verb. It doesn't need "do" for questions or negatives. *"She is happy"*, not *"She does happy"*.

**Articles** — the tiny words "a", "an", "the" — are the biggest pain for Russian speakers because Russian doesn't have them. But they carry meaning: *"a doctor"* (any doctor) vs *"the doctor"* (that specific one).

**Present Simple** is your daily engine. *"I work"*, *"She works"*, *"They don't work"*. The third-person "-s" is the most common mistake Russian speakers make.

**Pronouns** connect your sentences. *"I saw him"* — not *"I saw he"*. *"This is mine"* — not *"This is my"*.

**Prepositions** map Russian cases to English. *"В Москве"* → *"in Moscow"*. *"На работе"* → *"at work"*. Each preposition is a bridge between the two languages.

**Countable/Uncountable** teaches you when to use "many" vs "much", "few" vs "little". English cares about whether you can count something.

**Adjectives & Comparison** — beautiful, more beautiful, the most beautiful. Simple rules, but the irregular ones (*good → better → best*) trip everyone up.

**There is/There are** — how English expresses existence. Not *"it is a book on the table"*, but *"there is a book on the table"*.

**Adverbs & Frequency** — always, usually, sometimes, never. Where do they go? Before the main verb, after "be".

**Modal Verbs** — can, must, should. The golden rule: they take a bare infinitive. *"She can swim"*, not *"she can swims"* or *"she can to swim"*.

**Questions** — the full system: what, where, when, why, who, how. Plus the tricky difference between *"Who called?"* and *"Who did you call?"*.

**Imperative & Let's** — commands and suggestions. *"Close the door"*, *"Let's go"*.

**Future** — will vs going to. Will for spontaneous decisions, going to for plans.

**Numbers, Time, Money** — the practical vocabulary you need every day.

---

By the end of this chapter, you'll have built the foundation. Every sentence in English will feel more natural. The garden is open. Let's walk through it.
