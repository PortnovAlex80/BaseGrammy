# Chapter 2 — Tenses and Structure of Speech

## The River of Time

Time flows through English like a river — and the tenses are the bridges across it.

You already know Present Simple from Chapter 1. Now you'll learn to navigate the past, the future, and the subtle shades between them.

**Past Simple** is your first bridge back. Regular verbs add "-ed" (*worked, played*), but the 60 most common verbs are irregular (*went, saw, bought, thought*). You need them by heart — there's no shortcut.

**Present Continuous** (*I am working*) is the tense of "right now" and temporary situations. But beware: stative verbs (*know, want, love*) NEVER take continuous. *"I am knowing"* is always wrong.

**Past Continuous** (*I was working*) paints the background. *"While I was reading, the phone rang"* — the continuous action was interrupted.

**Present Perfect** (*I have worked*) is the bridge between past and present. It's the tense that confuses Russian speakers most because Russian uses Past Simple for everything. *"I lived in London"* (finished) vs *"I've lived in London for 5 years"* (still there). This distinction is critical.

**Past Perfect** (*I had worked*) orders events in the past. *"By the time I arrived, she had left."* First she left, then you arrived. Without Past Perfect, the timeline is ambiguous.

**Passive Voice** shifts focus from "who did it" to "what was done". *"The report was written by Anna"* — the report matters, not Anna.

**Relative Clauses** connect ideas within a sentence. *"The man who called you is my boss."* Without them, your English sounds choppy.

**Gerund & Infinitive** — the choice between *"I enjoy reading"* and *"I want to read"*. Some verbs change meaning: *"I stopped smoking"* vs *"I stopped to smoke"*. These aren't small details — they change your message.

**Phrasal Verbs** are English's secret weapon. *"Turn on"*, *"figure out"*, *"carry out"*. Two or three words that mean something completely different from their parts.

**Conditionals** — the world of "if". Zero conditional (facts), First (real possibilities), Second (unreal present), Third (unreal past), Mixed (past → present). Mastering conditionals means mastering hypothetical thinking in English.

**Modal Perfects** — *"should have"*, *"must have"*, *"could have"*. Judging the past. *"You should have told me"* — you didn't, and I'm not happy about it.

**Reported Speech** — how to relay what someone said. *"She said she was tired."* The tense shifts back, pronouns change, time words adjust.

**Connecting Words** — the mortar between your sentence bricks. *However, although, despite, moreover, therefore*. These make your English flow naturally.

---

By the end of this chapter, you can talk about any time, any condition, and any connection between ideas. The river of time is yours to navigate.
