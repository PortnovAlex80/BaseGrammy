# Chapter 0 — Before Language

---

In the beginning there was light.

Not the kind that comes from somewhere. The kind that simply *is* — diffuse, sourceless, the way light exists in dreams before you notice it and it disappears. It filled the world the way water fills a glass: completely, without effort, without anyone deciding it should be so.

Two beings lived inside this light. We will call them Iro and Ua, because those were the first sounds they made — not names chosen but sounds that escaped, the way a breath escapes when you've been holding it too long.

They did not know each other. Not yet. They knew only the light, and the warmth of the light, and the way the world looked when the light was in it.

Then the light began to go.

---

It didn't happen all at once. That would have been easier, in a way — a sudden dark you could point at and name. Instead it was gradual. A slow withdrawal, like a tide that goes out so gently you only notice when you look down and the water is gone and your feet are standing on cold sand.

Iro noticed first.

There was a place on the far edge of the world where the light had been brightest. Iro had looked at this place every morning — if morning still meant something, if time still had divisions — and felt something that we would now call *reassurance*, but which Iro experienced simply as the absence of fear. The place existed. The light was there. Everything continued.

One day the light in that place was less than it had been.

The next day, less again.

Iro stood very still and watched this and felt the fear arrive — not the absence of reassurance, but the presence of its opposite. Something was happening. Something that moved in one direction only. Something that, if it continued, would take everything that the light had made visible and return it to the state before visibility.

The state before *known*.

Inside Iro, a thought began to form. Not a word — there were no words yet, not in the way we mean. But the shape of a thought. The architecture of something urgent that needed to cross the space between one mind and another.

The problem was: there was no bridge.

---

Ua had words. Of a kind.

Not sentences — sentences require a speaker and a moment and a direction. What Ua had were infinitives. Pure, undated, unowned actions floating in the air like seeds that haven't found soil.

*{it}Vedere{/it}.* To see.

*{it}Sapere{/it}.* To know.

*{it}Restare{/it}.* To stay.

Ua held these the way you hold stones in your hand — aware of their weight, uncertain what to build with them. *{it}Restare{/it}* was the one Ua returned to most often. It had a quality the others didn't. A kind of resistance. A refusal of movement. To stay. To remain. To be-here-rather-than-gone.

Ua didn't know yet what it meant to stay for someone. Only what it meant to stay.

When Iro and Ua first became aware of each other — across the dimming space of the world, two presences in a light that was slowly forgetting itself — something passed between them that wasn't language. A recognition. The particular feeling of knowing that another being is watching the same disappearance you are watching.

Iro sent a word across the space.

*{it}Sparire{/it}.*

To disappear.

Ua received it. Understood it the way you understand cold — immediately, in the body, without translation. *{it}Sparire{/it}*. Yes. That was what was happening. The word named the motion of the light, the direction of things, the quality of this particular fear.

But *{it}sparire{/it}* was an infinitive. It had no subject. It didn't say *what* was disappearing, or *when*, or whether there was anything to be done about it. An infinitive is a fact without a frame. You know something is true inside it. You don't know where to put it.

Ua sent back: *{it}Restare{/it}.*

To stay.

Iro held this word. Felt its weight. *{it}Restare{/it}* — yes. That was the response to *{it}sparire{/it}*. That was the shape of what was needed. But *{it}restare{/it}* was also frameless, subjectless, timeless. It didn't say *who* should stay, or *where*, or *when it was still possible*.

They stood in the fading light, these two beings, trading infinitives like people passing stones across a distance. The stones arrived. Nothing was built.

---

Here is what Iro could not yet say:

*If I had known then that you would disappear, I would have stayed beside you. And the world that we both saw would not have gone out.*

This sentence — this exact sentence — existed inside Iro from the beginning. It was not a vague feeling. It was a specific, structurally complete thought. It had a condition in the past. It had a consequence that didn't happen. It had a shared memory between two people. It had a world held inside a relative clause. It had sorrow and precision in equal measure.

But to say it, Iro would need tenses that didn't exist yet. Pronouns. Conjunctions. Moods. A subjunctive for the hypothesis. A conditional perfect for the unrealized consequence. A relative pronoun to hold the shared world inside the sentence like a photograph inside a frame.

Iro would need, in other words, almost everything.

The light continued its slow withdrawal.

Ua stayed on one side of the darkness. Iro on the other. Between them: two infinitives, insufficient for everything that mattered, and the long work of building a language complex enough to hold the truth.

*{it}Sparire{/it}.*

*{it}Restare{/it}.*

The world said nothing.

It was waiting, as things that are disappearing will, for someone to find the words in time.
