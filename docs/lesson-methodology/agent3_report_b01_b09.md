## Key Finding: Old ZIP Duplicates

**CRITICAL**: Old ZIP lessons L03 and L05 are **identical files** (same content, different titles "Imperfetto" vs "Futuro + Remoto"). Likewise L04 and L06 are **identical files** (same content, different titles "Passato Prossimo" vs "Condizionale + Mix"). ## Lesson B01: Imperfetto (past habits, states, background)

- **New file**: lesson_17_B01.csv
- **Old ZIP mapping**: Old L03 (titled "Imperfetto") -- 150 sentences total, but only ~30 use Imperfetto. The remaining ~120 are Presente Indicativo sentences that happen to share the same vocabulary.
- **Sentence count**: Old 30 Imperfetto sentences (out of 150 total) vs New 41 sentences (100% Imperfetto)
- **Coverage comparison**:
  - New B01 is 100% focused on Imperfetto. Every single sentence uses an Imperfetto form.
  - Old L03 is 80% filler Presente sentences, only 20% Imperfetto. Vocabulary words like *casa, lavoro, tempo, vita, amico, donna, famiglia, scuola, acqua, cuore* are repeated across tenses without pedagogical purpose.
  - New covers all 6 persons (io/tu/lui/lei/noi/voi/loro) systematically.
  - New covers all 3 conjugation groups: -are (*giocavo, guardavamo, ascoltavamo, comprava*), -ere (*scriveva, sorrideva, correva, piangeva*), -ire (*capivo, dormivano, sentivo*).
  - New includes key irregular Imperfetto: *ero, avevo, sapeva, volevo, potevo, stava, facevo*.
  - New covers all target functions: habits (*andavo sempre, ogni sera*), states (*era felice, stava sulla collina*), background (*quando sono arrivato, dormivano*), ongoing past processes (*pensavo a te, scriveva una lettera*).
- **Notable differences**:
  - Old L03 has NO consistent Imperfetto practice. A student seeing "Io ho molto tempo libero" (Presente) in an "Imperfetto" lesson is pedagogically confusing.
  - New B01 has natural, everyday sentences appropriate for A2 level: *Quando ero bambino, giocavo fuori. Mia mamma preparava pranzi deliziosi. L'autobus arrivava alle 8 del mattino.*
  - Old uses clunky word-by-word glosses in parentheses: *(bambino, giocavo, fuori)* mixed with Russian translations that over-specify. New has cleaner format.
  - Old L03 repeats identical vocabulary across Presente/Imperfetto with no clear pedagogical pattern (e.g., "Lui è un buon uomo" / "Era un uomo comune" / "Lui è un buon padre").
- **Gaps**:
  - Old missing: structured coverage of all 6 persons in Imperfetto, clear time markers (*ogni giorno, sempre, da bambino, mentre*), contrast with PP.
  - New missing: nothing significant for an introductory Imperfetto lesson. Sentence count (41) is within the 60-70 target range lower bound; could be expanded slightly.

## Lesson B02: Participio Passato (V3 forms)

- **New file**: lesson_18_B02.csv
- **Old ZIP mapping**: No dedicated old lesson. Old L04 has some participio forms embedded in PP constructions but never isolates the participio as a standalone item.
- **Sentence count**: Old 0 standalone participio sentences vs New 40 sentences
- **Coverage comparison**:
  - New B02 is entirely novel -- no old equivalent exists. This lesson isolates participio passato as a building block before PP is introduced.
  - New covers regular forms systematically: -ato (*fatto, preparato, comprato, cambiato, passato*), -uto (*perso, avuto, potuto, voluto, dovuto, saputo* -- note some irregulars), -ito (*finito, pulito, aperto, chiuso, scritto, visto, letto* -- note many are irregular).
  - New covers all key irregular participles: *fatto, detto, visto, letto, preso, messo, scritto, aperto, chiuso, nato, morto, venuto/arrivato, rimasto, tornato, diventato, uscito*.
  - First 20 sentences use participio as adjective (*E un lavoro fatto. E una porta aperta.*), teaching the form in isolation.
  - Last 5 sentences preview PP construction (*Ho finito il lavoro. Abbiamo scritto una lettera.*).
  - Middle section covers essere-group participles (*alzata, seduta, uscito, tornato, arrivata, morto, nato, rimasta, diventata*).
- **Notable differences**:
  - This is a completely new lesson type with no old equivalent. The old ZIP jumped straight from Presente to Imperfetto to PP without isolating participio.
  - The methodology note says "investment in all of Block B and C" -- this is correct. Participio is the building block for PP, Trapassato, Futuro Anteriore, Condizionale Composto, Congiuntivo Passato, Infinito Passato, Gerundio Passato.
- **Gaps**:
  - Old: complete gap -- no isolated participio practice.
  - New: Sentence count (40) is below the 50-60 target. Could add more participio-as-adjective examples and more irregular participles (*scelto, volto, rotto, sciolto, spento, acceso*).

- **Coverage comparison**:

  - New covers regular participles thoroughly: -ato (*pranzato, preparato, studiato, comprato, pagato, lavorato, giocato*), -uto (*venduto, avuto, potuto, voluto, dovuto, saputo*), -ito (*finito, dormito, capito*).

  - New missing: *voi* person forms (avete + participio) are absent. Sentence count (41) is below the 60-70 target. Could add: *non...ancora* construction (*Non ho ancora finito*), *già* with PP (*Ho già mangiato*), more *voi* examples.

## Lesson B05: Passato Prossimo vs Imperfetto

- **New file**: lesson_21_B05.csv
- **Old ZIP mapping**: No dedicated old lesson. Old L03 and L04 contain mixed Imperfetto/PP sentences but never present them as minimal pairs for contrast.
- **Sentence count**: Old 0 contrast pairs vs New 38 sentences (19 minimal pairs)
- **Coverage comparison**:
  - New B05 is entirely novel. The old ZIP never presented PP vs Imperfetto as a pedagogical contrast.
  - New uses 19 minimal pairs showing the same verb in both tenses:
    - PP = specific event (*Ieri ho fatto i compiti*) vs Imperfetto = habit (*Da bambino facevo i compiti la sera*)
    - PP = completed action (*Ha aperto la finestra*) vs Imperfetto = state (*La finestra era aperta*)
    - PP = punctual event (*Ieri e arrivata alle otto*) vs Imperfetto = background (*Arrivava sempre in tempo*)
    - *Mentre* + Imperfetto / *Quando* + PP pattern
  - Covers the core narrative skill: *Mentre lei dormiva, io lavoravo* (background + background) vs *Quando si e addormentata, ho iniziato a lavorare* (event triggers new action).
- **Notable differences**:
  - This is the single most important lesson for narrative competence and it did not exist in the old ZIP at all.
  - The minimal pair structure is pedagogically excellent: students see the same vocabulary used in both tenses and learn to distinguish function, not form.
  - Old L03+L04 together contain PP and Imperfetto sentences but never place them side by side.
- **Gaps**:
  - Old: complete gap -- no PP vs Imperfetto contrast practice.
  - New: Sentence count (38 = 19 pairs) is below the 60-70 target. Could add more pairs, especially with *mentre* clauses and *quando* clauses as explicit triggers. Missing: third-person plural contrasts, negative contrasts (*non ho ancora vs non ancora*).

  - New covers all persons with *avere* auxiliary: *avevo finito, aveva già visto, avevamo cenato, avevano venduto*.

### Old ZIP Unique Lesson Content

The old ZIP has 3 unique lessons relevant to B-block (L03, L04, L07), totaling 585 sentences. However:
- **~140 sentences** (24%) actually use the target tense
- **~445 sentences** (76%) are Presente/Imperfetto/PP filler using the same vocabulary
- L05 = L03 (identical file), L06 = L04 (identical file) -- 0 additional unique content
- L06 title "Condizionale + Mix" is fraudulent -- it is identical to L04 "Passato Prossimo" with zero Condizionale
- L05 title "Futuro + Remoto" is identical to L03 "Imperfetto" with zero Futuro

### Expansion Factor

- Old effective B-block content: ~140 sentences across 6 topics (many thinly covered)
- New B-block content: 360 sentences across 9 focused lessons
- **Expansion factor: 2.6x** focused content, plus 3 entirely new topics (B02, B05, B08)

### Key Observations

1. **Tense isolation is the single biggest improvement.** Old lessons mixed 3-5 tenses in a single lesson. New lessons are 100% focused on one target tense each. This alone makes the new curriculum dramatically more effective for learning.

2. **5 topics had zero or near-zero old coverage:** Participio Passato (0), PP vs Imperfetto contrast (0), Trapassato (4), Futuro Anteriore (0), Condizionale (0). The new curriculum covers all of these thoroughly.

3. **Old lesson titles were misleading.** L05 "Futuro + Remoto" was actually Imperfetto/Presente. L06 "Condizionale + Mix" was actually PP/Imperfetto. L07 "Futuro + Remoto" was a mixed-tense lesson with ~24% Futuro content. This means students using the old pack had no reliable path to learning Futuro, Condizionale, or Trapassato.

4. **Old vocabulary-driven approach vs new function-driven approach.** Old lessons organized by vocabulary (words like *casa, lavoro, tempo, vita*) repeated across all tenses. New lessons organize by grammatical function, using varied vocabulary to illustrate each tense.

5. **Sentence quality is significantly improved.** New sentences are natural, contextual, and pedagogically purposeful. Old sentences often felt like vocabulary drills with parenthetical glosses that overloaded the learner.

6. **PP vs Imperfetto contrast (B05) is the most impactful new lesson.** This is the core narrative skill and it did not exist in the old curriculum at all. The 19 minimal pairs teach students to distinguish background from event -- the foundation of all Italian storytelling.

7. **Sentence counts are below targets.** The plan specifies 40-70 sentences per lesson. Current B01-B09 average is 40.1 sentences. Most lessons could benefit from 10-20 additional sentences to reach target ranges, especially B01 (Imperfetto: 41 vs 60-70 target), B03 (PP Avere: 41 vs 60-70), B04 (PP Essere: 41 vs 60-70), B07 (Futuro: 42 vs 60-70), B09 (Condizionale: 40 vs 60-70).

8. **Missing person in new lessons.** B03 (PP con Avere) is missing *voi* (avete) forms. B09 (Condizionale) has minimal *voi* coverage. B01 (Imperfetto) could benefit from more *voi* examples.
